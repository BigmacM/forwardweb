Local SEO for a Markham or Richmond Hill business: what to fix first

For a Markham or Richmond Hill business, local SEO starts with a complete Google Business Profile and matching listings, and only then with your website. Here is the order I would work in.

The short version
  • Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. You can influence relevance and prominence, and you cannot move your address.
  • Fix in this order: Google Business Profile and categories, the same name, address and phone everywhere, reviews, one honest location page, then local links.
  • One page per town that repeats the same copy with the name swapped is the thing Google's spam policy warns about. Write one page that covers the region properly.

If you run a business in Markham, Richmond Hill, Thornhill or anywhere else in York Region, you are not competing with the whole internet. You are competing with a short list of businesses near the person searching. That makes local SEO more winnable than general SEO, and it also makes the order of the work matter more than the volume.

This is the order I would work in, with the reasoning for each step. I am based in Vaughan, so I will say what I know and where I am reporting what Google says rather than what I have tested myself.

How does Google decide who shows up locally?

Google explains it in three words on its help page about local ranking (checked 2026-11-03).

  • Relevance: how well your Business Profile matches what someone is searching for.
  • Distance: how far you are from the person searching.
  • Prominence: how well known you are, which Google says is based in part on how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have.

Only two of the three are in your hands. You cannot change where your shop is, so distance is fixed. Relevance and prominence are the work, and everything below maps to one of them.

What are people actually searching for?

For the size of the market, I checked. In Ahrefs data for Canada, pulled 2026-11-03, “local seo markham” has about 250 searches a month with a keyword difficulty of 0, and “seo richmond hill” has about 100, also at 0. Those are small numbers and an easy field, which is typical for local terms.

Those searches are from businesses looking for help with SEO, not from your customers. Your customers search for the thing you do plus a place, such as “dentist Markham” or “plumber Richmond Hill”. The same steps apply.

What should I fix first? Your Google Business Profile

For most local businesses, this is the highest-return page you have, and it is free. If you have a storefront or serve customers at a location, claim it and fill in all of it.

Three things trip people up, and all three come from Google’s guidelines for representing your business (checked 2026-11-03).

  1. Use your real business name. Google says the name should reflect your real-world name. Marketing taglines and unnecessary extra information are not allowed in the name.
  2. Your address has to be a real one. Google does not accept P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations. If you work from home and visit customers, you can hide your address and set a service area instead.
  3. One profile per location. Duplicates split your reviews and confuse everyone.

Which categories should I pick?

Choose one primary category that says what the business is, then only as many extras as truly describe it. Google’s guidance is to pick as few categories as possible, each as specific as possible, and to think “this business is a…” and not “this business has a…”.

The easy mistake is adding categories for services you offer occasionally. A broad spread dilutes the signal, and relevance is the thing categories feed.

Do reviews help local ranking?

Yes. Google states that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking, and it lists them under prominence.

What I would do is simple and boring. Ask every happy customer, at the moment they are happiest, and make it a one-tap link. Reply to every review, the bad ones too. Do not offer anything in return, and do not screen who gets asked: check Google’s current review policies before you build a process, because the rules are strict.

There is a second reason to care. A report in December 2025 said ChatGPT had begun showing local knowledge panels, and one source noted it appeared to mirror what is in Google Business Profile (Search Engine Roundtable, 2025-12-22). That is a single report and I would not build a strategy on it. But it points the same way as everything else, which is that a complete profile with real reviews helps in more than one place. I cover the wider picture in the guide to getting recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini and AI Overviews.

Why does the same name, address and phone matter?

Because conflicting facts make machines hedge. This is the NAP check: your name, address and phone should read identically on your website, your Business Profile and every directory you appear in.

  1. Write the canonical version once. Exactly how the name, street address with unit, and phone number should appear.
  2. Find every place it appears. Search your business name and your phone number. List the directories, social profiles and old pages that turn up.
  3. Fix the ones you control and claim the rest. Update or claim the listings that are wrong.
  4. Look for the old ones. A previous address or an old phone number is the usual culprit.
  5. Check again in a month. Some listings are slow to update.

I see this most with businesses that moved a unit or changed a number. The old one lives on in a dozen places, and that is exactly the problem described in how to check whether ChatGPT recommends your business.

Do I need a page for each town?

You need one good page, not five thin ones.

Google’s spam policies (checked 2026-11-03) describe doorway abuse as pages created to rank for specific, similar queries, and one of its examples is multiple domains or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one place. A page for Markham, another for Richmond Hill and another for Thornhill with the same text and a different place name is the pattern that policy describes.

What works instead is a single location page that is genuinely useful. Say which areas you serve and how you work there. Put in real local detail that is specific to you, such as the neighbourhoods you cover, projects you have done and the questions locals ask. If a section would read the same for every town, it does not belong.

On my own site there is one page for York Region that covers Markham, Richmond Hill and Thornhill together, and a separate page for Richmond Hill, since it is the town next door to me. I also have a page for Vaughan. That is close to the edge of what I would defend, so the test I apply is whether a page has something distinct to say. If it does not, merge it into the regional page.

How do I get local links?

Prominence includes links, and the best local links are the ones a real person would give you anyway.

  • Your suppliers and partners. Ask whether they list the businesses they work with.
  • Your chamber of commerce, business improvement area or industry association, if you belong to one.
  • Community sponsorships. A local team or event that links to its sponsors.
  • Local press and blogs, when you have something worth reporting.

Skip paid directories that exist only to sell links. One link from a genuine local organization is worth more than fifty from sites nobody visits.

What should I check once this is done?

Check indexing and impressions after a few weeks. A new location page can sit unindexed for a while, and what to check when only your homepage is indexed walks through it. If you see impressions and no clicks, impressions but no clicks explains how to tell whether it is position or snippet.

Local results move slowly. Compare the same weekday ranges and give changes several weeks before judging them.

FAQ

Do I need a website for local SEO if I have a Google Business Profile?

You can appear with a profile alone, but a website gives Google and assistants more to confirm who you are, what you do and where. It also gives your profile something to link to.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number. Google says more reviews and better ratings can help, so aim for a steady flow of recent, genuine ones rather than a burst.

Should I make a page for each of Markham, Richmond Hill and Thornhill?

Usually not. Separate near-identical pages risk looking like doorway pages under Google’s spam policy. One strong page that covers the region, with real local detail, is safer and more useful.

Does it matter if I work from home?

Yes, for the address. If customers do not visit you, hide your address and set a service area. Do not use a mailbox or P.O. box as a business address.
