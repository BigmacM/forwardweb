How much does a small business website cost in Toronto in 2026?

Published figures put a brochure site in the low thousands and a larger one in the tens of thousands. Here are the sources, what moves the price, and where I sit.

Maksut Ozcan

The short version
  • Published Toronto and Canadian bands for a small business site run from about $3,000 to $8,000 CAD, and higher for larger sites.
  • Care plans are published at roughly $50 to $250 a month. Mine is higher, and I say what it includes.
  • These are vendors' own figures, so treat them as planning bands, not quotes. Ask for a fixed price in writing.

If you have asked three agencies for a price and got three very different numbers, that is normal. There is no standard rate card for a website, and the range is wide because the work behind the word “website” is wide.

This note collects the published figures I could find, tells you where each came from, and then shows where my own prices sit against them. I checked these sources on 2 November 2026. They are vendors’ own blog posts, and the people publishing them sell this work, so read them as planning bands and not as quotes.

What the market publishes

Toronto, April 2026. Shift8, a Toronto web studio, publishes its pricing in a 2026 Toronto cost guide. It puts a brochure site at $3,000 to $7,500 CAD and a larger site of 6 to 20 pages at $7,500 to $20,000. It lists care plans at $75 to $250 a month.

Canada. Impeka publishes a Canadian pricing guide. It puts a small business site at $3,000 to $8,000 CAD and hosting and maintenance at $50 to $200 a month.

Those two agree reasonably well on the lower end. A simple site by a professional studio or freelancer in this market is a few thousand dollars, and a bigger one starts where the first band ends.

And for AI visibility work

This is a newer category and the figures are thinner and less settled.

  • WebFX publishes a guide to GEO pricing that puts small business retainers at $1,500 to $5,000 a month. That figure is in US dollars.
  • One published roundup, GTM 80/20’s audit cost guide, puts productized audits at $440 to $1,500 and consultant-led audits at $1,500 to $15,000. I have only partly read that roundup, so treat its numbers as a rough band.

I could not find a sourced Canadian-dollar figure for a local SEO retainer, so I am not going to quote one.

What drives the price

The same word covers very different jobs. These are the things that actually move the number.

  1. How many pages, and who writes them. Ten pages of finished copy is a different job from ten pages the studio has to write with you.
  2. Template or custom. A template with your logo is quick and cheap. A site designed around your customers and built by hand costs more and usually loads faster.
  3. What the site has to do. A page of text and a phone number is simple. Bookings, payments, a customer login or a quoting tool are software, and they price like software.
  4. Search and analytics set-up. Whether the build includes structure, speed, structured data and measurement, or leaves them for later.
  5. Integrations. Connecting to a booking system, a CRM or a payment provider adds work that depends on how good the other tool’s connection is.
  6. Who you are dealing with. One person usually has lower overhead than a team with account managers, and a team can cover more at once. Neither is automatically better.

I will be straight about the care plan. At $300 per month a month it sits above the figures I found. Maintenance plans vary a lot in what they include, so a monthly price on its own tells you almost nothing. Mine covers hosting oversight, updates and backups, uptime monitoring, a monthly report and up to two hours of small edits each month. Whatever plan you are offered, from me or anyone else, ask what it covers in writing before you compare numbers.

What to ask before you sign

  • Is the price fixed? An hourly estimate that “should” come in around a figure is not a quote.
  • What exactly is included? Pages, rounds of revisions, copy, photos, search set-up, training.
  • Who owns what at the end? You should own the content, the code and the accounts. Ask this one directly.
  • Who does the work? The person you speak to and the person who builds it are sometimes different people.
  • What happens after launch? Hosting, updates, fixes and what they cost each month.
  • How will you know it worked? A good answer names something you can see, such as enquiries or bookings, and says how it will be measured.

A price you cannot compare is a price you cannot trust.

How I’d decide

If your site is mostly an online business card, a few thousand dollars from a competent studio or freelancer is a sensible budget, and a template-based build can be a reasonable choice. If the site has to do real work, such as win customers from search, take bookings or run part of your operations, plan for more, and judge the quote by what it includes, not by how low it is.
