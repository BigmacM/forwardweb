Case study

From almost no traffic to a top source of bookings, found by Google and by AI.

A day-tour business I built and grew. Site, search and measurement, all me. Here is what moved the numbers, and what didn't.

The work itself, captured from the live site on 2 November 2026.

Three pages from the live site

Four stages, in the order I did them.

  1. BaselineMeasurement set up before anything else.
  2. FoundationFast, readable, indexable pages.
  3. ContentAnswers to what buyers actually ask.
  4. AI visibilityCrawlers let in, assistant referrals tracked.
Stage 1: Baseline

A new site almost nobody could find.

The business had a booking idea and a trickle of visitors. Search sent almost nothing. Before touching design I set up measurement, so every later change could be traced to a result.

Stage 2: Foundation

Fast, readable, indexable.

Technical SEO first, because nothing else counts without it.

  • Clean structure, one canonical per page, a real sitemap
  • Structured data for the business, tours and FAQs
  • Pages that load fast on a phone
Stage 3: Content

Answer what buyers actually ask.

Published on a schedule, around the questions people type before they book: cost, timing, what is included. Every post has a named author and a date.

Stage 4: AI visibility

Get named by the assistants.

I opened the site to AI crawlers on purpose, fixed what was blocking them, wrote short direct answers they can quote, and tracked assistant referrals as their own source in GA4. That last step is how I found out they were sending bookings.

The surpriseFewer visitors, much warmer.

Visitors from AI assistants booked at about five times the rate of organic visitors.

What didn't work

I would rather tell you now.

Paid lead forms bought the wrong people. Plenty of leads came in, almost none booked, so I stopped them and moved the money to search and content.
